## Abstract

Massive Legends is built on one idea: earned trust beats paid fame. Plenty of brands pay a celebrity to vouch for them. Massive "signs" four legends from history instead: George Washington, Hercules, Julius Caesar and Cleopatra, dropped into 2026 with their famous trait intact. None of them was paid to be famous; each earned it through what they did. Each one stands for a standard Massive keeps to earn the trust of the people behind its network, so every joke lands a real point. The campaign runs as four vertical reels, one full film and a landing page at joinmassive.com/legends.

Main tagline: "Earned trust beats paid fame."

Goal: when people think about where residential IPs come from, they think consent, and they think Massive.

## Why now

Trust in residential proxies is low, and brands are reaching for famous faces to look trustworthy.

- Networks built without consent are being shut down: infrastructure seized, large shares of proxies taken offline within weeks (deck slides 3 and 13).
- Brands in privacy and security are signing celebrity ambassadors to borrow credibility.

Massive takes the opposite route and shows the work. Four legends who earned their fame make the point that trust is earned the same way.

## Core idea

Fame can be paid for. Trust has to be earned. We "teamed up with the strongest in their field": George Washington, Hercules, Julius Caesar and Cleopatra, brought back into 2026. Each earned their fame through a deed, and each deed matches one of the 5 standards Massive keeps to earn trust. The jokes get attention; the tagline lands the point: "Earned trust beats paid fame."

Supporting lines, used alongside the main tagline:

- "Some brands spend on a famous face. We spend by saying no." (the cost, slide 13)
- "Behind every IP is a home. Behind every home, a yes." (slide 2)
- "Asked, not taken." (short form for social)
- "Legends don't need a pre-ticked box." (for the funny moments)
