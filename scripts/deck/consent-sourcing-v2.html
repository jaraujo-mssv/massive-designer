How residential IPs have been sourced.

Early days

Botnets, P2P apps and free VPNs routed traffic through users' devices.

Image: Proxyway, on Trend Micro's research
The SDK era

Apps were paid per install to embed proxy SDKs, often without telling users.

Image: Google Threat Intelligence
Now

Consent flows users can see: opt-in, pause, uninstall.

Three common consent dark patterns.

Hidden

No visible consent, basically a trojan.

Toolbars bundled into other installs, never clearly asked for.

Fake consent

Buried or vague language, no way to pause.

Avast's installer, with an extra browser pre-ticked and the terms in one small line.

Hostage

Consent shown, but runs as a system process with no uninstall.

Zango showed a license screen, then ran at every startup and left files behind that needed extra tools to remove.

Hidden.

Setup never mentions it. The app installs, launches, and a background process starts carrying other people's traffic.

How to spot it
  • No mention of bandwidth, IP address or an operator at install
  • Steady network use while the app sits idle
  • The only disclosure, if any, is in a privacy policy

Fake consent.

The ask exists, but it's built to be missed: a clause deep in the license, a box already ticked, words like "network optimization." Once you're in, there's no switch.

How to spot it
  • The box is ticked before you touch it
  • The operator isn't named
  • Vague terms: "partner services," "optimization"
  • No setting to pause it

Hostage.

The consent screen is real. What happens after isn't. The proxy installs as its own system service, and removing the app doesn't remove it.

How to spot it
  • A service running as Local System, set to start automatically
  • Uninstall greyed out, or it "succeeds" and the service stays
  • A separate uninstaller you have to go looking for

Why is it still worth it?

Networks built without consent carry legal and platform risk: app stores remove their SDKs, and law enforcement seizes their infrastructure. Consent-based sourcing avoids that exposure.

IPIDEA, Jan 2026

~40% of its proxies gone in the weeks after Google's action.

Alarum / NetNut, Jul 2026

Hundreds of domains seized by the FBI.

Standards to check providers against.

Five standards
01

Affirmative opt-in

Own screen, operator named, nothing pre-ticked.

02

One-step exit

Pause and uninstall on the device, nothing left running.

03

Partner accountability

Every SDK app reviewed, monitored, with a kill switch.

04

Buyer vetting before traffic

Identity, use case, payment and sanctions checks.

05

Published abuse data

Blocklist and rejection rates in public.

Best Practices for Consent-Based Residential Network Services, Jul 2026
Ask your proxy provider
  • Can you show me the exact consent screen a device owner sees?
  • Is the box pre-ticked? Is your company named on it?
  • How does a user pause sharing, and how fast?
  • What's left on the device after uninstall?
  • Which apps carry your SDK, and who reviewed them?
  • Have you ever cut off a partner? How fast?
  • What do you check before a new customer sends traffic?
  • Do you publish rejection and abuse numbers?
